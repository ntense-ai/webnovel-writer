## I. Common Tropes to Avoid or Reverse

| Trope | Problem | Frequency |
|------|------|---------|
| Homogeneous system stories with unrestricted advantages | Saturated market, little differentiation | Very high |
| Unlimited numerical growth | Readers lose anticipation and motivation to continue | Very high |
| Still acquiring the special advantage in chapter 2 | Slow opening and high abandonment | High |
| No clear opponent by chapter 3 | Insufficient conflict drives readers away | High |
| Excessive world explanation in the first 5 chapters | Deadly dullness; readers skip | High |
| No clear short-term protagonist goal | Readers do not know why to continue | Medium |
| Combining IP without new logic, merely copying | No creativity or surprise | Medium |

## II. Unconventional Constraints (Choose at Least One per Project)

### 2.1 System/Special-Advantage Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| GM-001 | Explicit time, usage, or energy cap | Resource management becomes central strategy | A trial card lasts 5 minutes, then needs 24 hours to recharge |
| GM-002 | Conditional activation, not always available | Creating the conditions drives the plot | The system activates only in extreme danger |
| GM-003 | Random negative effects | Risk accompanies rewards | Each use has a 20% chance of a random penalty |
| GM-004 | Discovery by others changes the game | Concealment and protection create tension | Once exposed, every player targets the protagonist |
| GM-005 | The advantage conflicts with game/world rules | Rule conflict creates sustained tension | A system-granted skill violates the game's physics |

### 2.2 Growth Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| GM-006 | Numerical growth has a ceiling requiring a breakthrough | Breakthroughs become stage climaxes | Level 100 cap; a special mission is required to exceed it |
| GM-007 | Upgrades demand real costs, not just grinding | Every gain has weight | Upgrading requires abandoning an existing skill |
| GM-008 | Strong abilities have side effects | Greater power means greater cost | The strongest skill causes a three-day coma |
| GM-009 | Resources are scarce, not unlimited | Competition becomes central conflict | Only 10 rare-material spawn points exist on the server |
| GM-010 | Higher levels bring greater threats | Strength does not guarantee safety | Reaching S rank causes S-rank monsters to hunt the protagonist |

### 2.3 Combat/Competition Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| GM-011 | No unlimited fighting above one's level | Find strategic openings | Challenging higher levels triggers system penalties |
| GM-012 | Victory costs resources, exposure, or debt | Winning is not free | Win the match but reveal a core tactic |
| GM-013 | Opponents can exploit rules too | Rules cut both ways | A discovered loophole is used against its discoverer |
| GM-014 | Inherit a predecessor's enemies | Growth brings new trouble | Defeating a powerful figure transfers their enemies to the protagonist |
| GM-015 | Certain game mechanics/physical laws are unbreakable | Firm boundaries make the world credible | No amount of strength overrides basic game physics |

### 2.4 World/Information Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| GM-016 | The protagonist lacks complete rules | Exploration sustains momentum | Only half the manual exists; the rest must be discovered |
| GM-017 | Rules evolve over time | Continuous adaptation | Weekly updates invalidate old strategies |
| GM-018 | Players/factions interpret rules differently | Information gaps create strategic contests | The protagonist discovers an interpretation others missed |
| GM-019 | Forgetting prevents unlimited knowledge accumulation | Information management becomes central | Each death erases some memories |
| GM-020 | Revealing or demonstrating an ability changes its rules | Concealment becomes survival strategy | Publicly using a skill causes a system nerf |

## III. Unconventional Payoffs (Alternatives to Humiliating Rivals)

### 3.1 Strategic Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| GM-S01 | Rule counterplay | Exploit loopholes against opponents | Use the game's adjudication mechanics to make an opponent self-destruct |
| GM-S02 | Behavioral prediction | Predict AI/NPC/opponent patterns | Analyze behavior and plan three moves ahead |
| GM-S03 | Cross-system transfer | Transfer knowledge across systems/games | Solve a game puzzle with real-world physics |
| GM-S04 | Weak beats strong | Weak resources defeat strong resources | Craft top-tier equipment from the lowest-grade materials |
| GM-S05 | Hidden mechanics | Discover and exploit concealed systems | Find a dungeon unknown to everyone else |

### 3.2 Cost-Based Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| GM-S06 | Costly victory | Win by paying a real price | Spend every resource for one crucial victory |
| GM-S07 | Weaponized side effect | Turn drawbacks into weapons | Reflect a skill's negative effect onto the enemy |
| GM-S08 | Optimal within limits | Find the best constrained solution | Defeat a boss using only beginner equipment |
| GM-S09 | Short-term sacrifice | Trade immediate gain for lasting advantage | Lose ranking deliberately for a better resource position |
| GM-S10 | Their own medicine | Defeat enemies with their rules | Turn an opponent's trap back on them |

### 3.3 Reversal Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| GM-S11 | Bug becomes core | A supposed bug becomes central combat strength | A mocked useless skill becomes the ultimate weapon |
| GM-S12 | Failure proves optimal | An apparent mistake is the best choice | The worst class has hidden advantages |
| GM-S13 | Rules overturned | Undermine the rules themselves | Discover that the game's underlying logic is wrong |
| GM-S14 | NPC awakening | NPC/AI shows unexpected complexity | A supposedly disposable NPC has personal goals |
| GM-S15 | Truth revealed | Reveal the game/world's nature | Discover the game mirrors the real world |

## IV. Combination Examples

### Example 1: Game Fiction with IP Fusion

```
Anti-trope rules: GM-005 (advantage conflicts with game rules) + GM-016 (incomplete rules)
Payoffs: GM-S01 (rule counterplay) + GM-S12 (failure proves optimal)

Core conflict: Transported into an IP world whose plot has diverged; the protagonist's spoilers grow unreliable
Creative driver: How can survival strategies adapt to a changing story?
Differentiation: Not “Knowing the plot makes me invincible,” but “The plot I know is increasingly wrong”
```

### Example 2: Apocalypse Survival and Construction

```
Anti-trope rules: GM-009 (scarcity) + GM-010 (greater levels, greater threats)
Payoffs: GM-S04 (weak beats strong) + GM-S09 (short-term sacrifice)

Core conflict: Crafting/building competes with other survivors for resources, while growth attracts stronger threats
Creative driver: How can a sustainable survival system be built amid scarcity?
Differentiation: Not “My system makes me invincible,” but “My system makes me everyone's target”
```

### Example 3: Hard Science Fiction and Cosmic Horror

```
Anti-trope rules: GM-016 (incomplete rules) + GM-017 (evolving rules)
Payoffs: GM-S05 (hidden mechanics) + GM-S15 (truth revealed)

Core conflict: A rational protagonist seeks patterns in an irrational world, but the patterns keep changing
Creative driver: How can rationality survive continuously evolving rules?
Differentiation: Not “I found the pattern, so I win,” but “The pattern itself is a trap”
```

## V. Usage Guide

### 5.1 Selection Process

1. Choose 1–2 unconventional constraints.
2. Choose 2–3 unconventional payoff patterns as the main modes.
3. Check whether their combination produces meaningful synergy.
4. Design 2–3 distinctive plot solutions arising from the constraints.

### 5.2 Game/Science-Fiction Notes

- ✅ **Introduce the special advantage in chapter 1**, not chapter 2.
- ✅ **Establish a clear opponent by chapter 3**: environment, rules, or a specific antagonist.
- ✅ **Make numerical feedback visible**, such as combat power 50 → 180.
- ✅ **Cap the advantage**: time-limited trial cards, resource-consuming abilities.
- ✅ **At least one small payoff per chapter**; do not leave a chapter without satisfaction.
- ❌ System dialogue over 1,000 English words; readers will skip it.
- ❌ Excessive world explanation in the first 5 chapters.
- ❌ Abilities with no ceiling or cost.

### 5.3 Pitfalls

- ❌ Choosing a constraint, then bypassing it with another cheat.
- ❌ Constraints that work only early and disappear later.
- ❌ Constraints that conflict with payoff patterns.
- ✅ Maintain constraints throughout.
- ✅ Let constraints drive the plot.
- ✅ Make constraints and payoffs reinforce each other.
