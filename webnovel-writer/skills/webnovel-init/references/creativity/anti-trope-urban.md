## I. Common Tropes to Avoid or Reverse

| Trope | Problem | Frequency |
|------|------|---------|
| Saturated entertainment-industry premise: transmigrate and instantly become a star | Severe oversupply and sameness | Very high |
| Cost-free advantage: system without consumption or limits | No tension or anticipation | Very high |
| Purely evil antagonist without motivation | Thin characterization and unconvincing conflict | High |
| Transmigrate and immediately master martial arts | Dated trope without knowledge-driven novelty | High |
| Chapter 1 is only dialogue, with no environment | Readers cannot immerse themselves | High |
| Flat female characters in romantic arcs | Risks damaging reader reception | Medium |
| Knowledge superiority without resistance or skepticism | No conflict; cheap satisfaction | Medium |

## II. Unconventional Constraints (Choose at Least One per Project)

### 2.1 Special-Advantage Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| UR-001 | Each use consumes resources, time, or health | Every use is a resource decision | Foreseeing the future consumes a year of life |
| UR-002 | Activation requires specific conditions | Creating conditions drives the plot | The system works only in desperate situations |
| UR-003 | Information advantage is limited to part of the future/history | Incomplete information sustains suspense | Only events within three days are visible |
| UR-004 | Discovery brings lethal danger | Concealment and protection create tension | Government agencies pursue the protagonist if the system is exposed |
| UR-005 | The advantage amplifies a core flaw with use | Ability and cost are deeply linked | Stronger foresight makes the present harder to experience |

### 2.2 Protagonist Capability Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| UR-006 | Knowledge expires as history diverges | Constant adaptation | One change derails all subsequent history |
| UR-007 | Expertise takes time to apply or monetize | Accumulation becomes the main arc | Know the formula but lack equipment and funding |
| UR-008 | Changing history/an industry causes chain reactions | Nonlinear results create unexpected conflict | Saving one person causes another disaster |
| UR-009 | A moral boundary limits exploitation of information gaps | Moral choices create tension | Know stock movements but refuse insider trading |
| UR-010 | Modern thinking is misunderstood in another era/context | Cultural conflict creates comedy and crises | Equality is considered treasonous in an ancient society |

### 2.3 Relationship Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| UR-011 | Allies have interests, not unconditional loyalty | Alliances need maintenance | A partner may switch sides for profit |
| UR-012 | Antagonists have reasonable motives | More convincing opposition | An industry giant protects entrenched interests |
| UR-013 | Romance faces genuine resistance | Relationships require preparation | The other person has a career and principles |
| UR-014 | Authorities require repeated proof | Recognition builds into a payoff | An expert needs three successes before believing |
| UR-015 | Success triggers envy and political resistance | Success creates new crises | Interest groups unite against a successful reformer |

### 2.4 World-Rule Limits

| ID | Anti-trope rule | Creative driver | Example |
|----|-----------|---------|------|
| UR-016 | History/industries have inertia; change takes time and cost | Reform becomes the main arc | A new technology needs ten years to transform a market |
| UR-017 | Resources are finite | Resource management becomes strategy | Only one chance at startup funding |
| UR-018 | Period limitations constrain the protagonist too | The protagonist is not omnipotent | Knowing a disease's cause cannot replace unavailable modern medicine |
| UR-019 | Success brings larger pressures | An ongoing game structure | A larger company faces stronger competitors |
| UR-020 | Certain legal, moral, or physical rules cannot be broken | Boundaries create credibility | Intelligence cannot override physical laws |

## III. Unconventional Payoffs (Alternatives to Humiliating Rivals)

### 3.1 Knowledge-Based Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| UR-S01 | Knowledge solves problems | Modern knowledge addresses historical/industry problems through visible reasoning | Show the chemistry behind solving an ancient smelting problem |
| UR-S02 | Predictive planning | Anticipate actions and prepare | A move planted three chapters earlier matters at the critical moment |
| UR-S03 | Rule loopholes | Discover and lawfully exploit gaps | Counter an opponent through a loophole in ancient law |
| UR-S04 | Cross-domain transfer | Apply field A's knowledge to field B | Modern management solves an ancient army's problems |
| UR-S05 | Information reversal | Possess crucial information the opponent lacks | Know the enemy's trump card while pretending ignorance |

### 3.2 Cost-Based Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| UR-S06 | Costly victory | Real sacrifice gives victory weight | Give up a relationship to gain a crucial ally |
| UR-S07 | Trade-off | Sacrifice something for something more important | Reveal a secret to earn trust |
| UR-S08 | Weaponized weakness | Turn a weakness into a weapon | Exploit an opponent's contempt to set a trap |
| UR-S09 | Breakthrough within rules | Find an opening inside constraints | Find a lawful route for women to trade within historical ritual law |
| UR-S10 | Accumulation pays off | Succeed through preparation, not innate talent | Ten chapters of groundwork pay off together |

### 3.3 Reversal Payoffs

| ID | Pattern | Description | Example |
|----|---------|------|------|
| UR-S11 | Underestimated counterattack | A precise response from the underestimated | Data proves everyone predicting failure wrong |
| UR-S12 | Apparent failure as strategy | A loss is part of a larger plan | Lose a negotiation deliberately for greater leverage |
| UR-S13 | Overturned understanding | The opponent realizes their error | Discover they have been guided by the protagonist all along |
| UR-S14 | Multi-faction impact | Influence several powers at once | One decision changes three factions' trajectories |
| UR-S15 | Historical revelation | Reveal a quietly transformed era/industry | Readers realize the protagonist has changed history's direction |

## IV. Combination Examples

### Example 1: Historical Transmigration and Knowledge-Driven Reform

```
Anti-trope rules: UR-006 (expiring knowledge) + UR-008 (historical chain reactions)
Payoffs: UR-S01 (knowledge solves problems) + UR-S14 (multi-faction impact)

Core conflict: The protagonist knows history, but every intervention changes it
Creative driver: How can the best solution be found in continuously changing history?
Differentiation: Not “Knowing the future makes me invincible,” but “The future I know grows less accurate”
```

### Example 2: Urban Industry Reform

```
Anti-trope rules: UR-007 (knowledge takes time to apply) + UR-015 (success provokes resistance)
Payoffs: UR-S10 (accumulation pays off) + UR-S03 (rule loopholes)

Core conflict: The protagonist can transform an industry, but entrenched interests block every step
Creative driver: How can a lawful path to change be found within the system?
Differentiation: Not “My system makes me invincible,” but “I defeat rules using rules”
```

### Example 3: Historical Transmigration with a Farming Heroine

```
Anti-trope rules: UR-009 (moral boundary) + UR-016 (historical inertia)
Payoffs: UR-S09 (breakthrough within rules) + UR-S11 (underestimated counterattack)

Core conflict: A heroine has modern knowledge but cannot act freely because of women's status in her era
Creative driver: How can she find a route to economic independence within ritual constraints?
Differentiation: Not “Transmigration lets me break every rule,” but “I found openings within the rules”
```

## V. Usage Guide

### 5.1 Selection Process

1. Choose 1–2 unconventional constraints.
2. Choose 2–3 unconventional payoff patterns as the main modes.
3. Check for meaningful synergy.
4. Design 2–3 distinctive solutions arising from the constraints.

### 5.2 Urban/Historical Notes

- ✅ **Show the knowledge process**: Not merely stating an answer, but demonstrating the reasoning.
- ✅ **Give antagonists reasonable motives**: Conflicting interests are stronger than simply bad character.
- ✅ **Create genuine romantic resistance**: The other person has principles and a career.
- ✅ **A peak every 3 chapters**: Chapter 1 predicament; chapter 2 first demonstration; chapter 3 small victory plus new resistance.
- ❌ Cost-free advantages or systems with no consumption.
- ❌ Unopposed knowledge superiority where everyone immediately believes.
- ❌ Pure evil without motivation.

### 5.3 Pitfalls

- ❌ Choosing a constraint and bypassing it with a cheat.
- ❌ Constraints disappearing after the opening.
- ❌ Constraints conflicting with payoff patterns.
- ✅ Maintain constraints throughout.
- ✅ Let constraints drive plot development.
- ✅ Make constraints and payoffs reinforce each other.
