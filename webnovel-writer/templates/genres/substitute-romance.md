# Substitute-Lover Romance Template

> Runtime preset identifier: `substitute-romance` (English taxonomy and initialization preset).

## Core Premise

### Essence of Substitute-Lover Fiction
```
Identity crisis + distinguishing genuine and false affection + pursuing self-worth
```

### Central Triangle
- **Heroine (the substitute)**: Struggles with identity and grows emotionally.
- **Male lead (the obsessed lover)**: Moves from fixation to genuine love.
- **Idealized former love (the original)**: A foil and source of conflict.

## Recommended Creative Constraints (Optional)
- Recommended constraint pack: Pack F16
- General overlay: Pack U01

## Heroine Types

### Passive Substitute
- Chosen to become a substitute.
- Inner conflict and self-doubt.
- Growth: acceptance → awakening.

### Deliberate Substitute
- Intentionally becomes a substitute for a purpose.
- Clear-eyed and strategic.
- Growth: exploitation → genuine feelings.

### Awakened Substitute
- Shifts from passivity to agency.
- A crucial awakening moment.
- Growth: losing herself → reclaiming herself.

### Reversal Substitute
- Identity reversal: the substitute was the true original love.
- The revelation is shocking.
- Growth: misunderstanding → truth.

---

## Heroine's Psychological Growth

```
Unaware → Suspicious → Certain → Resolute → Letting go
```

### Unaware
- Does not know she is a substitute, or knows but does not care.
- Immersed in being cherished.

### Suspicious
- Notices subtle clues.
- Begins doubting and feeling shaken.

### Certain
- Confirms her substitute status.
- Emotional collapse and shattered self-worth.

### Resolute
- Decides to leave, reclaim herself, and stop depending on him.

### Letting Go
- Truly makes peace and completes her personal growth.

---

## Male Lead's Psychological Change

```
Obsession → Confusion → Recognition → Remorse → Winning her back
```

### Obsession
- Consumed by his idealized former love.
- Treats the heroine as a substitute rather than seeing her.

### Confusion
- Begins confusing the two and developing real feelings for the heroine.
- Refuses to admit it.

### Recognition
- Recognizes that he loves the heroine, not a shadow of his former love.
- Struggles internally.

### Remorse
- Recognizes his mistakes and regrets hurting her.
- Wants to make amends.

### Winning Her Back
- A painful redemption-and-pursuit arc.
- Proves himself through actions and waits for forgiveness.

---

## Key Plot Beats

### Meeting (First 10%)
- The substitute enters the story.
- Establish the substitute relationship.
- Plant identity clues.

### Sweet Phase
- Everyday affection.
- Moments of attraction.
- Reader identification.

### Cracks Appear
- Substitute status is discovered/the original love appears.
- The relationship destabilizes.

### Truth Revealed
- Identity is fully exposed.
- Emotional collapse and relationship breakdown.

### Separation
- The heroine leaves.
- The male lead repents.
- Each grows independently.

### Painful Pursuit
- The male lead tries to win her back.
- The heroine refuses/hesitates.
- Repeated emotional push and pull.

### Ending Choices
- HE: reconciliation and happiness.
- BE: separate lives, wishing each other well.
- OE: open ending.

## Anguish Design

### Identity Anguish
- The moment she discovers she is a substitute.
- Being treated as someone else.
- Her self-worth is denied.

### Emotional Anguish
- Genuine devotion goes unseen.
- Being loved, but not for herself.
- The pain of unattainable love.

### Comparison Anguish
- Comparison with the idealized former love.
- Never becoming "that person."
- Even being better cannot surpass the original.

---

## Sweetness Design

### Sweet Everyday Life
- Small acts of care.
- Unconscious favoritism.
- Tenderness reserved for her.

### Sweetness of Awakening
- He discovers his true feelings.
- Recognizes her uniqueness.
- Begins truly seeing her.

### Sweetness of Pursuit
- His humility and effort.
- Thoughtful attempts to make amends.
- Sincere confessions.

---

## Suggested Settings

### Period
- Modern city/Republican era/ancient court.

### Social Setting
- Wealthy family/entertainment industry/ancient prince's household.

### Status of the Original Love
- Dead/missing/no longer in love/never actually existed.

### Sweetness-to-Anguish Ratio
- Early: 3:7.
- Middle: 1:9.
- Late: 7:3.

---

## Cautions

- The heroine must not forgive without boundaries.
- The male lead's pursuit must be sincere and difficult.
- The idealized former love cannot be too perfect, or replacement becomes implausible.
- Reveal the substitute identity dramatically.
- Emotional changes require a process; avoid abrupt shifts.
