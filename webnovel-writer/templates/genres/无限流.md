# Infinite-World Survival Fiction Template

> Runtime preset identifier: `无限流` (preserved for taxonomy and initialization compatibility).

> **Core appeal**: Clearing scenarios + solving rule-based puzzles + extreme survival. Grow through deadly games and break the rules with intelligence and courage.

## 1. Core Subgenres

### Horror Scenarios
- **Features**: Enter horror-film/urban-legend worlds; complete missions to leave.
- **Core payoffs**: Decoding rules, desperate escapes, outsmarting ghosts and monsters.
- **Representative works**: *Terror Infinity*, *Return from the Abyss*, *I Became a God in a Horror Game*.

### Rule-Based Horror
- **Features**: Every scenario has unique rules; breaking them means death.
- **Core payoffs**: Discovering, exploiting, and turning rules against enemies.
- **Representative works**: *Lord of Mysteries*, *Global Martial Arts*, *My House of Horrors*.

### Team Competition
- **Features**: Multiplayer teams clear scenarios; teammates may be enemies.
- **Core payoffs**: Teamwork, betrayal reversals, psychological contests.
- **Representative works**: *Battle Royale*, *Alice in Borderland*.

### Overgod Space
- **Features**: An explicit overgod/system administers the space; points purchase abilities.
- **Core payoffs**: Ability combinations, resource management, cross-world adventures.
- **Representative works**: *Terror Infinity*, *Reincarnation Paradise*.

## 2. Worldbuilding: Infinite-Space Society

### Player Ecosystem
- **Newcomers**: Extremely low survival rates; veterans use them as expendable pawns.
- **Middle ranks**: Some strength; form regular teams.
- **Elites**: Understand core rules and can solo difficult scenarios.
- **Administrators**: Mysterious beings who make the rules.

### Resource System
- **Points**: Clearing rewards used for abilities/items.
- **Items**: Single-use consumables that save lives at critical moments.
- **Skills**: Permanent abilities requiring many points.
- **Intelligence**: Tradable scenario guides, a hard currency.

### Scenario Difficulty
- **D rank**: Beginner scenario; 70% survival.
- **C rank**: Normal; 50% survival.
- **B rank**: Difficult; 30% survival.
- **A rank**: Nightmare; 10% survival.
- **S rank**: Hell; 1% survival.

## 3. Scenario Design Framework

### Three Essential Elements
```
1. Background: The scenario world's basic rules.
2. Clear conditions: Explicit victory objectives.
3. Death conditions: Penalties for breaking rules.
```

### Structure of One Scenario
```
Entry (10%):
- Teleport into the scenario.
- Receive initial information/rule hints.
- Identify teammates/enemies.

Exploration (30%):
- Gather clues.
- Test rule boundaries.
- Someone dies after violating a rule, demonstrating its force.

Breaking the Deadlock (40%):
- Discover a loophole.
- Plan how to clear the scenario.
- Encounter surprises during execution.

Settlement (20%):
- Meet the clear conditions.
- Receive rewards.
- Return to the central space.
```

### Classic Scenario Types
| Type | Features | Examples |
|------|------|------|
| Survival | Stay alive until time expires | Zombie siege, deserted island |
| Puzzle | Find the truth/exit | Escape room, haunted-house exploration |
| Confrontation | Defeat a boss/enemy | Monster hunt, faction war |
| Mission | Complete a specific objective | Escort an NPC, collect items |
| Narrative | Change/complete a plot | Film worlds, historical scenarios |

## 4. Classic Payoff Patterns

### Turning the Rules Against an Enemy
- **Scene**: An enemy believes they understand the rules and tries to use them to kill the protagonist.
- **Reversal**: The protagonist discovers a deeper rule and exploits it instead.
- **Example**: The enemy says, "Seeing the ghost kills you"; the protagonist realizes, "Close your eyes and you cannot see it."

### Desperate Comeback
- **Scene**: Every teammate is dead and the protagonist faces the boss alone.
- **Explosion**: Saved trump cards/discovered loopholes reverse the outcome.
- **Key**: Trump cards must be foreshadowed, not appear from nowhere.

### Intelligence Advantage
- **Scene**: The protagonist knows the guide in advance through rebirth/intelligence trading.
- **Payoff**: Others are still exploring while the protagonist heads straight for the objective.
- **Limit**: Intelligence may be outdated/wrong; clearing cannot be mindless.

### Ability Awakening
- **Scene**: A life-or-death crisis awakens a new ability.
- **Payoff**: The ability perfectly fits the current predicament.
- **Limit**: Awakening carries a cost afterward (points/side effects).

## 5. Outline Structure (Standard: 2 Million Chinese Characters)

### Volume One: Beginner Zone (Chapters 1-50)
- **Core**: First scenario establishes understanding of the rules.
- **Growth**: From novice to competent player.
- **Climax**: Narrowly clear the first scenario and gain a core ability.

### Volume Two: Team Formation (Chapters 51-150)
- **Core**: Join/form a regular team.
- **Growth**: Learn teamwork and establish an ability build.
- **Climax**: The team's first B-rank challenge.

### Volume Three: Rising Through the Ranks (Chapters 151-300)
- **Core**: Stand out among players.
- **Growth**: Master multiple abilities and investigate the nature of the rules.
- **Climax**: Solo an A-rank scenario.

### Volume Four: Seeking the Truth (Chapters 301-500)
- **Core**: Explore the truth of the infinite space.
- **Growth**: Encounter administrator-level secrets.
- **Climax**: Discover the space's true nature.

### Volume Five: Endgame (Chapters 501-700)
- **Core**: Challenge/change the infinite space's rules.
- **Growth**: Become a rule-maker.
- **Climax**: Break the infinite space and gain freedom.

## 6. Teammate Design

### Classic Team Composition
| Character | Role | Function | Fate |
|------|------|------|------|
| Veteran | Mentor | Teaches the rules | Mid-story sacrifice |
| Newcomer | Foil | Demonstrates growth | May turn dark |
| Strategist | Support | Analyzes rules | Offers a crucial plan |
| Fighter | Damage dealer | Direct confrontation | Exits after a standout moment |
| Traitor | Reversal | Creates conflict | Defeated by protagonist |

### Teammate Death Pacing
- **Early**: Expendable teammates die to establish tension.
- **Middle**: Important teammates sacrifice themselves for emotional impact.
- **Late**: Core teammates face danger but can be rescued.

## 7. Ability System Design

### Ability Categories
| Type | Features | Examples |
|------|------|------|
| Combat | Direct damage | Fire, lightning, enhancement |
| Support | Buffs/debuffs | Healing, acceleration, weakening |
| Exploration | Information gathering | X-ray vision, foresight, sensing |
| Special | Rule interference | Time stop, space, causality |

### Acquisition Methods
- **Scenario rewards**: Random/chosen abilities after clearing.
- **Point exchange**: Purchases from the central-space shop.
- **Special opportunities**: Hidden rewards inside scenarios.
- **Ability fusion**: Combine abilities into advanced forms.

---

## 8. Common Pitfalls

### Must Avoid
- ❌ Contradictory scenario rules.
- ❌ Unlimited stacking of abilities until the protagonist becomes godlike.
- ❌ Tool-like teammates whose deaths mean nothing.
- ❌ Repetitive scenarios with only cosmetic changes.

### Recommended Practices
- ✅ Make each scenario's rules internally consistent.
- ✅ Define ability ceilings and costs.
- ✅ Give teammates independent personalities and storylines.
- ✅ Vary scenario types and increase difficulty progressively.
